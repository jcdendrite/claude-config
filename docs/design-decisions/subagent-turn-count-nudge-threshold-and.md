# Subagent turn-count nudge threshold, and a renewed rejection of a hard per-dispatch cap

*2026-08-24. Formerly `docs/design-decisions.md` §61.*

`nudge-long-turn-subagent.sh` (`claude/.claude/hooks/`) nudges — informational only, never blocking — when a subagent dispatch's own turn count crosses a measured outlier threshold. The threshold, 340, was chosen from a corpus scan of every `subagents/*.jsonl` transcript across this machine's personal-account corpus (4,058 dispatches with recorded turns): p50 = 28 turns, p90 = 82, p95 = 136, p99 = 339, max = 4,178 (`.claude/plans/prevent-runaway-subagent-cost.md`'s corpus-measurement bullet). 340 sits just above that measured p99, so the hook nudges only the same roughly-1%-outlier tail the corpus scan identified, not ordinary long-running work. The incident that motivated this mechanism (see `.claude/plans/prevent-runaway-subagent-cost.md`'s Context section for the full narrative) would have crossed this threshold after roughly 13% of its eventual turn count.

The hook checks and fires on a sampled cadence (every 10th `PostToolBatch` fire, `LONG_TURN_NUDGE_SAMPLE_CADENCE`) rather than every one, and tracks the running turn total incrementally via a small per-dispatch state file rather than a full-transcript rescan on every fire — both chosen to bound the `tail`/`jq -s` scan's cost specifically, from O(fire count) to O(fire count / `SAMPLE_CADENCE`), across every subagent dispatch system-wide, not just this one. That bound covers the scan alone, not total per-fire hook cost: the input-parsing `jq` call and the invocation-counter's `wc -c` read are each their own `_lib_capped_for`-wrapped fork that still runs on every subagent fire regardless of sampling, and total per-fire cost scales linearly with raw fire count via those two always-on forks. `mkdir -p "$MARKER_DIR"` forks only on a fire that finds the directory missing — the common steady-state fire skips it once the directory exists from the dispatch's first fire. Only the `MARKER_DIR` cleanup sweep is gated to the sampled fire alongside the scan. It fires at most once per dispatch (no re-arm, no escalation ladder): unlike `nudge-handoff-near-context-cap.sh`'s hard-block-after-N-ignored-re-arms design, a turn-count nudge is a coarse "check this is still on track" signal a human or orchestrator acts on once, not a per-turn budget to keep re-litigating.

A hard per-dispatch turn or time cap — killing a dispatch outright at some ceiling — was considered and rejected again here, the same conclusion [§10](check-runner-charter-scoping.md)–[§11](code-writer-self-review.md) already reached for review/fix work generally. `check-runner`'s `maxTurns: 20` ([§10](check-runner-charter-scoping.md)) was justified only by a narrow, deterministic charter (run checks, no setup, no write access) where a cap cannot truncate legitimate work; it was still retired 2026-06-23 after a follow-on measurement showed it continuing to misbehave (`docs/case-studies/check-runner.md`'s Retirement section, 649 sessions, 2026-05-22–2026-06-23). `code-writer` ([§11](code-writer-self-review.md)) deliberately carries no turn cap because its charter — like the review/fix loop this plan's incident exposed — is open-ended, and is backstopped by a downstream review pass instead of a ceiling. A dispatch legitimately reviewing, fixing, and re-reviewing a large or contentious change can reach several hundred turns without anything having gone wrong; a hard cap would kill that dispatch exactly as readily as a genuinely runaway one, with no way to tell the two apart from turn count alone. The nudge surfaces the same signal to a human or orchestrator instead of acting on it unilaterally.

`MAX_SCAN_WINDOW_BYTES`'s default, 2,000,000 bytes, bounds a single scan attempt's `tail | jq -s` window so a retry after a timed-out `jq -s` call re-reads a fixed-size slice rather than one that grows with however much backlog piled up across skipped sampling fires. The default is grounded against safely-fast `jq -s` parsing, not against catch-up rate: a 15 MB synthetic slice of similarly-shaped JSONL parsed in 0.15s in local measurement, well under the 2s `_lib_capped_for` cap. The catch-up rate (`MAX_SCAN_WINDOW_BYTES` / `SAMPLE_CADENCE` vs. transcript growth per fire) has not been measured against real transcript growth — see this hook's own "Known gaps" comment for that residual exposure.
