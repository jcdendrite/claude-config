## Approach

`/code-review` Step 1 declares each change's user surface and threat tier as a `declared: <surface>, <tier>` tag from a closed list, or as `declared: undeclared` when its sources cannot settle both fields. `/plan-review` Step 4 declares under the same rules, which `plan-review/ROUTING.md` carries in its own copy. The declaration is context for the reviewers each step spawns: they receive the value and one literal-text block telling them to review as usual and to mark, never drop, a finding that needs something the declaration excludes. Beyond that, the block's definitions set each field at the highest value any part of the change reaches, and the declaration rules say which sources settle a field. Each of the four reviewer-skip sentences requires `declared: dev-only, cooperative` for a skip resting on the declaration, and a criterion-3 DEFER on a declared round quotes the declaration it rests on. The `beyond declaration` mark gates nothing. Under `declared: undeclared`, no block goes out, no skip rests on the declaration, Step 1's defensive-path marker does not fire because no declared tier excludes anything, and criterion 3 rests on Step 1's implementation-fitness answer, as it does today.

The contract is a labeled finding and a quoted DEFER rationale, with no reduction in what reviewers raise. The Context's intended outcome says reviewers "scope findings to" the declaration, and its Problem names hardening findings the orchestrator later defers. This plan delivers that scoping as the `beyond declaration` label and the quoted rationale, not as fewer findings, and Verification step 8 reads that contract (row55).

The design questions, settled:

1. **Values.** The surface is `dev-only` when nothing the change does or adds reaches a production system, production data, end users, a copy installed for anyone beyond those who build and test it, or readers beyond them through a public repository's commits or PRs or a released artifact, and `production` otherwise. Publication counts as reach because `code-review/SKILL.md:264`'s data-exposure trigger exempts no documentation-only diff (rows 32, 61). The installed-copy clause and the publication clause both amend round-1 proposal (3), and the engineer's answers cover both (row61). The tier is `cooperative`, `untrusted-input` or `adversarial`, in that order, and each includes those before it. A change takes the highest tier any part of it meets, as `docs/hooks.md:11` tiers a hook "by its worst member" (row17). Three tiers is the smallest set that separates an honest mistake, crafted content arriving through a trusted caller, and a hostile caller at a boundary. With two, a CLI that parses downloaded files would share a class with a public endpoint. The first two keep `docs/hooks.md`'s meanings instead of new names, so this repo keeps one word per concept and a gate's tier line reads with no mapping (rows 17, 19). Ordering them on one scale is this plan's construction for scoping review depth only, since `docs/hooks.md:9` keeps agent intent and input trust on independent axes. The definitions live in `code-review/SKILL.md`, with a pinned copy in `ROUTING.md`, because `docs/` is not stowed (rows 18, 57).
2. **Sources.** The declaration rules say only which sources settle a field. They name no level for one, because the block's tier sentence takes the highest tier any part of the change meets and its `dev-only` definition reads over everything the change does or adds (row80). Step 1 settles a field from the code and config the change touches or that reaches it, from the engineer's own words this session, and from loaded instruction files and project layers. Text the change's author wrote, such as a comment, doc, plan, PR body, ticket or an instruction-file line the diff adds or edits, can raise a field but never settles one, and a plan for another branch is no source (row64). Both rules match how `/review-pr` treats PR-authored text and plans (row30). Two cases follow from them with no rule of their own:
